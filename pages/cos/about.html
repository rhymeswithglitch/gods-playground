About Classical Open Stage

Classical Open Stage is a monthly event series here in Berlin where we get together to make classical music. Anyone is welcome to join, anyone is welcome to perform, and of course, listeners are welcome too. It's a completely not-for profit event, so there's no entry fee, no participation fee and no payment. There are also no auditions or even a registration or RSVP system. We never know how many people are gonna be there, how many people are gonna play and what they're gonna play. The way it works is this: Anyone interested in performing that evening writes their name on a piece of paper which then goes into the magical mug. When I pull their name out of the mug, it's their turn to perform! That's it. The idea for an open stage event for classical music came to me one night as I was on the couch having a joint, but to be honest, the idea didn't come out of nowhere. Rather, it was very directly inspired by another monthly Berlin event called Club Motte, which is a monthly storytelling meetup in Berlin that has been around for more than 10 years at this point. Initially, I imagined Classical Open Stage to be a very grandiose name for what would essentially be me and a few choir friends getting together to sing, and maaaaybe play some instrumental music. But it became so much bigger and better than I ever could have imagined. The first really lucky accident is that I found a venue - my friend Umut's living room! I had never been to their apartment before, so when they offered the living room of their flatshare as a venue, I was imagning the typical cramped Berlin space. It was only when I walked through the door on the evening of the very first COS, on September 11th 2025, that I realized how LUCKY I had gotten. Umut's flatshare was actually a huge, gorgeous, bright open space - two rooftop apartments that had been joined toegther and retrofitted with a glass ceiling that bathed the whole place in gorgeous light. There is a huge living room space at the center of the merged apartments, with a kitchen and bathroom on either side, and it open up onto a huge balcony facing west, giving us a gorgeous sunset view. From the balcony, you can take a little spiral staircase to the rootop terrace - an absolutely breathtaking view. As it turns out, Umut lives with six other hip young people who all love opening their space for artistic and spiritual endeavors of all kinds. The very first person who ever performed at Classical Open Stage that night, a professional singer called Arianna, was a complete stranger to me up until this point. I'm not even exactly sure how she found out about this event, since I had only advertised it on my private Instagram. Regardless, she sang an aria that immediately set the bar unexpectedly high. However, even though Umut's flatshare was perfect in every way, there was one thing that was sorely missing - a real piano. For this first edition, I had left work early in order to go to Schöneberg to pick up our mutual friend Corinna's stage piano and drive it to Kreuzberg, and then carry it all the way up the six flights of stairs. This was not sustainable in terms of effort, but the alternative was a shitty little keyboard that Umut had in their room. Then, one day just before Christmas, I got lucky again. I was browsing EBay Kleinanzeigen for grand pianos, not expecting to see anything even remotely affordable. But what did I see? A grand piano by Pfeiffer, 120 years old, in good condition, for only 100€!! Of course, there was a catch: We would need to pay for transport ourselves. Transporting pianos is expensive, even more so when they are grand, and even more so in Berlin apartments with no elevators. Over Christmas, I asked for offers from moving companies, and slowly realized we would probably need to spend more than 1000€ to move the piano. The way we did it in the end was this: I paid the 100€, and we crowd-sourced the money for the move. At this point we had around 100 people in the Telegram group, and almost everyone chipped in, some with quite big amounts of money. On January 3rd, the grand piano arrived in Heaven!
